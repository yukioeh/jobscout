# Tailoring Guide — How to Customize the Master Resume

The master resume is built for the Sales Enablement + AI Transformation lane. For any given role, you change three things and leave the rest alone. Budget twenty minutes per application, not two hours.

---

## The three things you change

**1. The section header under your name.** It currently reads SALES ENABLEMENT & AI TRANSFORMATION LEADER. Change it to echo the job title in the posting. If the role is "Director, GTM Enablement," make it GTM ENABLEMENT & AI TRANSFORMATION LEADER. This is the single highest-leverage edit and it takes five seconds.

**2. The first sentence of the summary.** Lead with whichever proof point maps to their top requirement. Adoption? Lead with 85%. Revenue? Lead with $76.3M. Platform strategy? Lead with the 17-platform consolidation.

**3. Bullet order and swap-ins.** Reorder so the first bullet under each role is the one closest to their top requirement, and swap in from the library below. Never add bullets without removing some — two pages is the ceiling.

## Your five signature stories

Everything above is documentation. These are what actually get you hired. Each should be tellable in ninety seconds, in situation → action → result form, and each should have a version for "influence without authority," "a failure or setback," and "biggest impact."

**1. 88 virtual studios.** Scale, budget influence, org design, and it created a permanent new organization. Your best answer for "tell me about the largest thing you've run."

**2. 17-platform consolidation.** Systems strategy, cross-functional negotiation, measurable adoption lift. Your best answer for "how do you handle competing stakeholders."

**3. The SAP AI community.** Culture change from zero, no budget, no mandate. Your best answer for "influence without authority" and the story that most differentiates you.

**4. ServiceNow AI Sales Coach.** 85% adoption across 6,000 people in nine months. Your best answer for "biggest recent impact" and the freshest proof you have.

**5. Funding through influence.** Securing budget from regional orgs for centralized programs. Use this whenever someone probes the lack of direct budget ownership. Deliver it as a credential, not a caveat.

## Two things to prepare separately

**The departure answer.** You resigned on paper, so your verbal story has to be consistent with employment verification. Rehearse until it's boring:

> I left ServiceNow at the end of August. The organization shifted direction from what I was hired to build, and we agreed a package and a clean exit made sense for both sides. I'm proud of what we shipped in nine months — 85% adoption on the AI Sales Coach across 6,000 sellers and $76M in influenced pipeline — and I'm looking for the next place to do that with more runway.

**The team size answer.** Seven is a small number and you'll be asked:

> Seven direct, but the programs I ran reached 6,000 sellers across ten regions. Most of my career has been scaling through influence rather than headcount — that's what SAP's expert track is built for. I'd be glad to grow a larger team, and I'd point to the fact that I've had to earn cooperation rather than assign it.

## Before you send anything

- Verify the $76.3M, $45M NACV, 426 opportunities, and 85% figures are numbers you can defend from memory. You no longer have system access.
- Lock in three references this week with personal cell numbers and personal email addresses. Reference relationships decay fast after you leave.
- Have someone read your separation agreement for non-compete, non-solicit, and agreed departure language before you take a consulting client or repeat the departure story above.

## Search strategy

At Director level, portal applications have poor odds. Build a list of 30 target companies and map a path into each through your SAP and ServiceNow networks. Thirty mapped introductions will outperform 200 applications, and the work is roughly the same.

Keep the Director-level search at full effort in parallel with JPMorgan. That role is a stretch with a referral, which is worth pursuing hard, but it isn't a plan.
